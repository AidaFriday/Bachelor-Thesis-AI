\section{Motivation}

Face recognition has become an integral component of many modern applications, ranging from smartphone authentication and social media photo tagging to access control and video surveillance. As these systems increasingly influence security–critical and privacy–sensitive domains, the reliability and robustness of the underlying face recognition models are of central importance. Small differences in model performance can have significant practical consequences, for example in terms of false acceptances, false rejections, or biased behaviour across different conditions.

In recent years, a large number of open–source face recognition models have been proposed, such as FaceNet, ArcFace, and AdaFace, many of which provide high accuracy on standard benchmarks. However, these models are often evaluated in isolation, using different experimental setups, datasets, and metrics. This makes it difficult for practitioners to understand the real trade–offs between them, especially when models must operate under realistic constraints such as limited computing resources or the need for real–time processing. A systematic and fair comparison of well–known models, implemented in a common framework such as PyTorch, is therefore needed to support informed model selection for practical applications.:contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

Moreover, existing benchmark results are usually reported offline and do not provide an easy way to explore model behaviour interactively. Developers and researchers who want to test models on their own hardware or with their own data often need to implement ad–hoc scripts and tools, which is time–consuming and error–prone. A user–friendly application that integrates state–of–the–art models, standard datasets, and real–time webcam input into a single interface would lower the barrier to entry and make it easier to study the relationship between accuracy, latency, and resource usage.

This thesis is motivated by the need for transparent, comparable, and practically relevant evaluation of face recognition models. By benchmarking several widely used PyTorch implementations on standard datasets and embedding them into an interactive runtime benchmarking application, the work aims to provide both a systematic empirical study and a tool that can support future research and real–world deployment.


\section{Task}
The central task of this thesis is to design and implement a framework for systematic benchmarking and real-time analysis of face recognition models. Building on existing PyTorch implementations of well-known open-source models, the work aims to provide both a thorough empirical comparison and a practical tool that can be used to explore their behaviour under realistic conditions.:contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

Concretely, the thesis is structured around three main tasks:

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Evaluation of face recognition models:} Implement a unified interface for several pretrained models (e.g.\ FaceNet, ArcFace, AdaFace) in PyTorch and evaluate their performance using standard verification benchmarks. This includes computing common metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall and ROC/AUC on datasets like LFW, CFP-FP and YTF.
	
	\item \textbf{Benchmarking of runtime performance:} Measure and compare the computational characteristics of the selected models, including latency per frame, achievable frame rate (FPS) and memory usage on the available hardware. The goal is to understand the trade-offs between recognition accuracy and real-time capability.
	
	\item \textbf{Development of a runtime benchmarking application:} Design and implement a PyQt-based desktop application that integrates the selected models and exposes their functionality through a user-friendly graphical interface. The application should allow users to choose a model, load images or enable a webcam stream, and observe both recognition results and live performance metrics in real time.
\end{itemize}

By completing these tasks, the thesis will deliver (i) a consistent benchmarking setup for face recognition models, (ii) a comparative analysis of their accuracy and runtime properties, and (iii) a reusable application that can support further research and practical deployment decisions.
